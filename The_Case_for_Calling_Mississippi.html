The Case for Calling Mississippi

By Benjamin Rouan

Mississippi U.S. Senate 2026 · Scott Colom (D) v. Cindy Hyde-Smith (R) v. Ty Pinkins (I) · Figures as of Friday, Nov. 6, 2026, 6:45 PM CST · Prepared for the Associated Press

Thesis: Scott Colom's lead cannot be overcome by the ballots that remain. The race is callable now.

Colom leads by 2,574 votes on AP's count,24 and by 2,821 once county-published batches that AP has not yet loaded are included (Section 1).21,22,23,25 AP's own expected-vote figures put about 30,300 votes still out.24 For Hyde-Smith to win, those votes would have to break 54% her way (Section 5). Four independent lines of evidence say they will not, and most say they will do the opposite:

  1. AP's own remainder favors Colom. Apply each county's current vote share to AP's county-by-county estimate of what is left, with no adjustment for ballot type. The remaining vote nets Colom roughly +3,300 (Section 2).24 Remove most of Hinds County's remainder, as the county's records suggest,3,4,7 and it still nets Colom about +900.
  2. Affidavit ballots are breaking for Colom everywhere. Fifteen counties have added post-election batches (Section 3).13,14,15,16,21,23,24,25 In every one, Colom beat his own share of that county's vote, by 4 to 30 points. The ten confirmed affidavit batches split 65%–35% for Colom. That includes counties Trump carried by 50 points.16
  3. Late mail is too small and too evenly spread to matter. About 2,850 mail ballots are still outstanding statewide (Secretary of State, Nov. 6).1 Only 43% of them were sent to counties Hyde-Smith leads (Secretary of State county report, Nov. 2).8 At county leans they net Colom a few hundred votes (Section 4). Historically, only a small fraction arrive in the five-day window: Hinds counted 18 late mail ballots against 1,093 unreturned in 2024.4
  4. Hinds County can only widen the margin. AP shows Hinds at 93% reporting, about 5,800 votes out, in a county Colom leads 80%–19%.24 The county's precinct data indicate its absentees were already counted,3,4 and the commissioners' own district counts put the true remainder at roughly 1,500–2,500, nearly all affidavits.7 If AP's figure is right instead, Colom's final margin grows to about 1.1–1.2 points (Section 6f). Either way, the uncertainty in Hinds is only about how large Colom's win is.

A simulation of every outstanding pool, county by county (Section 5), gives Colom better than a 99.9% chance of winning. Even with affidavit lean set to zero, so that every remaining ballot votes exactly like its county, he wins in 99.7% of runs. Hyde-Smith wins only if remaining affidavit voters are substantially more Republican than their own counties, the reverse of every batch reported so far (Section 5).

Hyde-Smith netted votes only in Alcorn, Itawamba and George, counties Trump carried by 50 to 65 points in 2024,28 and even there by less than her overall margin. The Mississippi Free Press noted that across Alcorn, Chickasaw and Itawamba she netted just 71 votes "despite holding substantial overall leads in two of those counties."16

The pattern has a known cause. Mississippi moved more than 200,000 voters to inactive status before the election and requires inactive voters to vote by affidavit.19,26 A DeSoto County commissioner said "more than half" of the affidavits she reviewed were from previously inactive voters who were reactivated and whose ballots will count.11 Those voters are not a random sample of their county.

4. Late mail cannot close the gap

  • How many: just under 2,900 mail ballots (2,857) were still outstanding Friday afternoon,1 down from about 3,300 Thursday night. These are ballots sent out and not returned. Those postmarked by Election Day can arrive through 5 PM Tuesday, Nov. 10.
  • How many will arrive: few. Hinds' certified 2024 results show 18 "5 Day Absentee" ballots4 against 1,093 unreturned on the Secretary of State's Nov. 4, 2024 absentee report,27 under 2%. Even at ten times that rate, late mail adds a few hundred votes statewide.
  • Where they are: by the Secretary of State's Nov. 2 county report, 43% of unreturned ballots were in counties Hyde-Smith leads and 57% in counties Colom leads.8 At county leans (AP feed),24 if all 2,857 arrived, they would net Colom about +300.
  • The ceiling: even if every unreturned ballot in a Hyde-Smith county arrived and voted for her, and none in Colom's counties did, she would net about 1,230 (43% of 2,857),1,8 less than half the lead.

5. Hyde-Smith's path, tested

With about 30,300 votes left24 and Pinkins taking about 2%, Hyde-Smith needs 54.3% of the two-party remainder. In the affidavit batches so far (Section 3) she has won 35%. She has exceeded 54% only in Alcorn (63%), Itawamba (74%) and George (69%), three small counties that together hold under 500 of the remaining votes.24

6. Hinds County: AP's 7% outstanding is very likely too high, and it does not matter for the call

AP shows Hinds at 93% reporting: 77,465 counted against 83,296 expected, so about 5,800 out. AP's expected-vote parameters for Hinds are 74,951 Election Day in-person, 8,083 advance or absentee, and only 262 provisional.24 In other words, AP's remainder assumes thousands of regular ballots are still uncounted, and almost no affidavits. The county's records point the other way on both.

a. The precinct data show absentees are already counted

Hinds' 2024 certified precinct report splits every precinct into Election Day and absentee votes.4 Absentee shares ranged from 1.6% to 31.5%. If 2026 absentees were missing from the election-night report,3 precincts heavy with absentee voters would look badly short against 2024. They do not:

Turnout relative to 2024 is flat at 86–91% in every group. That is what counted absentees look like. Missing absentees would show as a steady decline down the table. Raymond 1, the most absentee-heavy precinct (31.5% in 2024), stands at 81% of its 2024 turnout; for its absentees to be uncounted, its 2026 Election Day turnout would have to exceed the 2024 presidential election by 18%. A regression across all 107 matched precincts, controlling for partisanship, puts the share of 2026 absentees already counted at 100% (90% interval 84–123%).

b. State rule: absentee scanning starts the moment polls close

"After the close of the polls at 7:00 p.m., the Resolution Board will open the ballot box containing the accepted absentee ballots … and shall immediately begin processing the accepted ballots through the central scanner. … The totals shall then be combined with the precinct tabulation totals for the unofficial vote count."

Mississippi Administrative Code, Secretary of State Rule 731c, Part 17 Rule 3.2 (e)–(f).5 Envelope review may begin when the polls open (3.2(a)). The rule also lets the board "recess as necessary and resume" before certification (3.2(g)), so it sets the expected practice rather than compelling it. The precinct data are the evidence that Hinds followed it this year.

c. The county described its post-election work as late mail and unreadable ballots

"The Hinds County Resolution Board began reviewing and processing absentee ballots at noon Nov. 4. That process includes eligible mail-in ballots received after Election Day, as well as ballots that cannot be properly read by scanning equipment."

WLBT, Nov. 4, 2026, reporting the Hinds County Election Commission's statement through board attorney Tony Gaylor.6 Hinds had 8,070 absentees returned by Nov. 2 and 639 not returned;8 mail "received after Election Day" can only come from the latter.

d. What is actually left in Hinds

Affidavits, which the county has not yet released. Commissioners have given counts for three of five districts: about 650 in District 5, 387 in District 3, and 136 from 5 of District 4's 14 precincts.7 Scaled to all five districts, that is roughly 2,300 cast. District 1 Commissioner Kidada Brown has said the county will not "turn a number in until everyone is complete."7 At the acceptance rates seen elsewhere, Hinds' affidavits yield about 1,300–1,800 votes. With a few hundred late mail ballots, the realistic remainder is 1,500–2,500, not 5,800. It is also far more than the 262 provisionals in AP's Hinds parameters.

e. Why the higher estimate is understandable

  • 2023 precedent. On election night in the 2023 governor's race, Hinds reported only 447 absentees and counted the rest the next day.17 A model trained on that behavior would expect the same in 2026. The 2026 election-night report uses a different template with no vote-type columns, and the precinct test above shows the absentees are in.
  • The "exceeded 2020" statement. The commission said preliminary figures indicated participation exceeded the November 2020 presidential election.6 Hinds cast 100,944 ballots in 2020.30 Even adding every absentee and affidavit to the current 77,465 would not reach that. The statement cannot be literal and should not be read as evidence of a large uncounted pool.
  • Turnout against the rest of the state. Statewide, the 2026 count stands at 77.6% of the 2024 presidential vote.24,28 Hinds is at 89.2%, right at the median for comparable Black-majority counties. Another 9,000 votes would put Hinds at 99.6% of its presidential-year turnout, the highest of all 82 counties.

f. Either way, Hinds helps only Colom

If AP's 5,800 were right24 and split like the rest of Hinds (Colom 80–19), Colom's final margin would rise to roughly +1.10 to +1.18 points. If the county's own data are right, Hinds adds about 1,000–1,500 net for Colom. Neither scenario offers Hyde-Smith anything. A model that keeps the race uncalled because 7% of Hinds is outstanding is holding the call on votes that can only extend the leader's margin.

What is uncertain, stated plainly

  • Acceptance rates vary. Chickasaw 51%, Tippah 48%, Itawamba 66%, Rankin about 80% so far, Lowndes about 90%.10,16 The large Republican-leaning pools (Rankin, Harrison, Jackson) have not finished. Lower acceptance shrinks both candidates' gains; it does not change direction.
  • The statewide affidavit total is not known. Published estimates run from 12,000 to 20,000-plus.19 Counted county figures already total about 15,800 in counties holding half the state's vote.7,9,10,11,12,13,16,21 A larger pool helps Colom at the leans observed; a smaller pool leaves Hyde-Smith fewer votes to recover with.
  • Five of the 15 batches are unlabelled (marked *). The ten confirmed batches tell the same story.
  • Cures. Wrong-precinct affidavits cannot be cured.10 Photo-ID affidavits and some voters registered through the Department of Public Safety can be. Cures can only raise acceptance, and the ID group is small: 365 of 21,421 affidavits in 2024.20
  • Hinds' election-night practice in 2024 could not be checked (no archived copy). The 2026 conclusion rests on the 2026 precinct data, the county's own description of its work, and the state rule.
